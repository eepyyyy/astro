Mandi & Gulika — The Sub-planets

In South Indian astrology, Mandi (மாந்தி) and Gulika (குளிகன்) are two highly malefic sub-planets (Upagrahas). They do not have physical bodies, but rather represent mathematical shadow points in space associated with the planet Saturn.

Mandi represents poison, obstacles, and blockages, while Gulika is associated with delays, debts, and structural struggles.

1. Dividing the Day into 8 Parts (Kalas)

To compute Mandi and Gulika, the duration of the daytime (sunrise to sunset) or nighttime (sunset to sunrise) is divided into 8 equal parts called Kalas.

During the day, the first 7 parts are ruled by the lords of the weekdays starting with the day's ruler, and the 8th part is lordless. Saturn's assigned Kala index differs depending on the weekday:

2. The Formulas

The formulas to find the exact Julian Day for Mandi and Gulika are:

  • Gulika is calculated at the **start** of Saturn's Kala.
  • Mandi is calculated at the **end** of Saturn's Kala.
Daytime:
Kala duration = (Sunset - Sunrise) / 8
Gulika Julian Day = Sunrise + (Saturn Index - 1) * Kala duration
Mandi Julian Day = Sunrise + Saturn Index * Kala duration

3. Calculating their Zodiac Longitudes

Once we have the exact Julian Days (jg for Gulika, jm for Mandi), their zodiac longitudes are equal to the **physical Ascendant (Lagna)** at those times.

Remember to calculate the Ascendant using swe.houses() and manually subtract the Lahiri Ayanamsa to convert the tropical longitude to sidereal.

Python Code Example

# Saturn indices (0-based) for Mandi/Saturn Kala
MANDI_DAY   = [5, 4, 7, 3, 2, 1, 6]  # Sun=0, Mon=1...
MANDI_NIGHT = [0, 1, 4, 2, 3, 6, 7]

def calculate_mandi_gulika(sr: float, ss: float, weekday: int, lat: float, lon: float) -> dict:
    # Get Saturn's Kala index
    mk_d = MANDI_DAY[weekday]
    
    # Mandi time (end of Saturn's Kala)
    jm = sr + mk_d * (ss - sr) / 8.0
    
    # Gulika time (start of Saturn's Kala)
    jg = jm - (ss - sr) / 8.0
    
    # Helper to compute sidereal Ascendant at a given JD
    def _asc(jd_val):
        cusps, ascmc = swe.houses(jd_val, lat, lon, b'P')
        aya = swe.get_ayanamsa_ut(jd_val)
        return (ascmc[0] - aya) % 360

    return {
        "mandi_lon"  : _asc(jm),
        "gulika_lon" : _asc(jg),
    }
